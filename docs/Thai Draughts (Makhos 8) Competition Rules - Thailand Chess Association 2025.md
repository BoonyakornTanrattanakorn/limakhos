## Chapter 1: Fundamentals of Playing 8-Piece Thai Draughts

### 1.1) General Provisions

**Article 1.** 8-piece Thai Draughts (*Makhos 8*) is a sport played between 2 players moving pieces on a square checkered board. Both sides take turns making 1 move at a time, with the **light-colored side moving first**. A player who has no pieces remaining, no legal moves remaining, or resigns loses the game and the game ends. In 1 round of competition, a match is played in paired boards (2 games, 4 games, or 6 games).

**Article 2.** The 8-piece Thai Draughts board is a square grid of 8 squares per side (8 × 8), totaling 64 squares, consisting of 32 dark squares and 32 light squares.

**Article 3.** 8-piece Thai Draughts is a competitive board game with 16 pieces in total on the board, divided into 8 dark-colored pieces and 8 light-colored pieces.

---

### 1.2) Board Setup

**Article 4.** For the starting position before play begins, the two players sit facing each other with the board placed centered between them. The 8 pieces per side are placed on the dark playable squares of the back rank and the second rank (4 pieces per rank). The **light-colored pieces move first** and occupy the **low-numbered side** (`1–8`).

### 1.3) Movement and Capturing

**Article 5.** A piece that is a **Man (*Bia*)** moves diagonally forward 1 square at a time. A Man cannot move backward.

- *Diagram (Man movement):* A light Man on square `2` can move diagonally forward 1 square to either `7` or `6`.

A piece that is a **King (*Hot*)** can move forward or backward along any of the 4 diagonal directions and may be placed on any vacant square along the line. A King may capture in multiple jumps and in multiple ways, and a player may choose any capture line without being required to take the line with the maximum number of captures—that is, if there are 2 capture paths available (such as a 2-piece capture or a 3-piece capture), the player may capture in either direction. A piece becomes a King through promotion, which occurs when a Man is moved to a square on the opponent's back rank; the opponent places a second piece of the same color stacked on top of it as the symbol of a King.

- *Diagram (King movement):* A light King on square `15` can move any distance along all 4 diagonals (`19–24–28`, `18–22–25–29`, `11–8–4`, and `10–6–1`).

**Article 6.** When pieces obstruct each other (i.e., an opponent's piece stands adjacent in an available capture direction with an empty square immediately behind it), the obstructing piece must be captured: the player picks up their own piece that is blocked, places it on the empty square immediately beyond the obstructing piece, and removes the captured piece from the board.

- *Diagram (Single capture by a Man):* Light Man on `1` jumps over dark Man on `6` and lands on `10`.
- *Diagram (Double capture by a Man):* Light Man on `1` jumps over dark Man on `6` to `10`, then jumps over dark Man on `15` to `19`.

A Man may make multiple consecutive captures. However, when a Man captures into the promotion rank (to become a King), it must first be the opponent's turn before it can continue capturing as a King, as illustrated.

- *Diagram (Capture ending on promotion rank):* A light Man makes consecutive captures to the opponent's back rank at square `30` (via `14 → 23 → 30`). Even though an enemy piece sits on square `25` along the adjacent diagonal toward `17` (marked with an `X`), the newly promoted piece cannot continue capturing as a King on the same turn.

When capturing with a King, if a piece obstructs a square along a diagonal that the King can reach, the King must capture by landing on the empty square **immediately behind** the obstructing piece; if another piece obstructs the King's path from that landing square, the King may continue capturing consecutively (a multi-capture), as illustrated.

- *Diagram (Double capture by a King):* Two examples show a King capturing along a diagonal, landing on the immediate vacant square behind the captured piece, and then changing direction along a diagonal to make a second consecutive capture.

**Article 7.** If a player touches a piece, that player must move that piece only. If a player touches one piece and then moves a different piece, it constitutes an irregularity, and the player must move the first piece touched.

**Article 8.** If a player performs the action in Article 7, but the touched piece has no legal move, it shall not be considered an irregularity.

**Article 9.** A player's move is completed when the player releases their hand from the piece. Regardless of which squares the piece may have touched along the way, only the act of releasing the hand from the piece is considered—once released, the move cannot be changed.

**Article 10.** If a player wishes to adjust the position of pieces on the board, they must ask the opponent's permission first, and may only request to do so during their own turn.

---

### 1.4) Win, Loss, and Draw

**Article 11.** A loss occurs in 4 cases:
1. The player has no pieces left.
2. The player has no legal moves left.
3. The player resigns.
4. The player is forfeited (penalized with a loss).

**Article 12.** If either player fails to appear at the start of the match, the opponent may start the clock and wait. Once the clock runs out for 2 games (boards), the absent player is forfeited across all games of the match.

**Article 13.** If both players repeat the same moves back and forth 3 times, producing the same board position 3 times (threefold repetition), that game is declared a draw.

**Article 14.** If both players maneuver back and forth—specifically, moving Kings around without any capture or Man move occurring—for **16 full moves**, that game is declared a draw.

**Article 15.** A draw may occur **only** under Article 13 and Article 14. Players may not agree to a draw by mutual consent, even if the position appears deadlocked or blocked; the game must be played out until a draw condition is met.

---

### 1.5) Counting to Claim a Draw

**Article 16.** Counting may be invoked when one side wishes to claim a draw while at a disadvantage in either material or clock time. The player may count **16 moves**. If either side moves a Man or makes a capture, the count resets and must restart from zero.

**Article 17.** When beginning a count, a player may start counting without notifying the arbiter, or may pause the clock to call the arbiter to observe the count.

**Article 18.** The player against whom the count is being made may concede a draw at any time during the 16-move count.

**Article 19.** The player who initiated the count may request to stop counting before 16 moves have been completed.

## Chapter 2: Competition Rules for 8-Piece Thai Draughts

### 2.1) The Game Clock

**Article 20.** Players must press the clock themselves using the **same hand** with which they moved the piece—that is, if moving with the left hand, press the clock with the left hand; if moving with the right hand, press the clock with the right hand. Neither the arbiter nor any third party may remind a player to press the clock.

**Article 21.** When a player notifies the arbiter that the opponent's time has expired, or the arbiter makes that ruling directly, the game ends immediately and the player whose time expired loses that board regardless of how many pieces remain.

**Article 22.** A player must wait for the opponent to complete their move and press the clock before making their own move and pressing the clock.

**Article 23.** Players must not pause the clock on their own without valid cause. For example, if a player needs to use the restroom, they must let their clock continue running and use their own time.

A player may pause the clock when needing to file a protest with the arbiter on grounds for which the arbiter has authority to issue a warning.

---

### 2.2) Irregularities and Penalties

**Article 24.** A **warning** is issued for a breach of competition rules. Only the opponent may protest directly to the arbiter; spectators, third parties, or the arbiter may not protest on a player's behalf. The arbiter issues Warning #1; once a player receives **3 warnings** in that game, the arbiter forfeits that player for that game.

Offenses carrying a warning penalty include:
1. Making an illegal move in violation of Chapter 1 (Section 1.3), or committing the conduct specified in Article 25(1).

**Article 25.** Offenses for which the arbiter may issue either a verbal caution or a suspended warning for the next infraction—provided the same standard is applied consistently throughout the tournament and announced to players in advance before the competition begins—are:
1. Failing to press the clock with the same hand used to move the piece on that turn.
2. Making loud noises or disturbing players under any circumstances.
3. Failing to record more than 5 moves (not necessarily consecutive; one light move plus one dark move counts as 1 full move), whether reported by the opponent or observed directly by the arbiter, who may issue a warning without waiting for a protest. The player must bring their scoresheet completely up to date to the current move on their own clock time before continuing play. This also includes intentionally recording inaccurate moves for more than 5 moves.

**Article 26.** Offenses resulting in **immediate forfeiture without prior warning**:
1. Using any communication device during play, or having a mobile phone ring or audibly vibrate during a game. The player is forfeited in that board, though they may continue in subsequent rounds. For this offense, the arbiter may forfeit the player directly without waiting for an opponent's protest.
2. Proven cheating in the competition. In addition to forfeiture from the tournament, the offender shall be referred to the Association for disciplinary review.

**Article 27.** A player has the right to protest to the arbiter to have the opponent warned once the opponent has made a move and pressed the clock. If the protest is made *before* the opponent presses the clock—or if the player realizes they made an illegal move *before* pressing the clock—the player must make a legal move using the piece already touched, unless that piece has no legal move, in which case another piece may be moved.

---

### 2.3) Recording Moves (Scoresheets)

**Article 28.** Players must record the game themselves accurately and systematically according to Chapter 3 (Section 3.1) Move Notation, and must keep the scoresheet up to date before making their next move.

The winning player must submit both players' scoresheets to the arbiter after every game. In the event of a draw, the **light-colored side** is responsible for submitting the scoresheets to the arbiter.

If a player has missed recording any moves, the arbiter shall require the player to fill in the missing moves on their own clock time until the scoresheet is complete up to the current move before continuing play.

**Article 29.** Players must keep their scoresheet visible to the arbiter at all times during play.

**Article 30.** Scoresheets are public records; players or other individuals may request to view or photograph the scoresheet of any game from the organizers.

**Article 31.** In competitions with a time increment of **5 seconds per move or more**, players must record moves until the end of the game; there is no exemption from recording when remaining time falls below 5 minutes.

The tournament organizer may specify in advance whether move recording is required or not required for an event.

---

### 2.4) Player Conduct

**Article 32.** Players must not act in any manner contrary to the good order and decorum of the competition.

**Article 33.** While a game is in progress, players must remain within the designated playing venue (the playing tables, restrooms, designated smoking/dining areas for active players, or other areas provided by the organizer) until their game in that round has concluded.

**Article 34.** A player must not leave their chair during their own turn unless permitted by the opponent or the arbiter.

---

### 2.5) Role of the Arbiter

**Article 35.** Arbiters must know the competition rules thoroughly and enforce them to maintain order throughout the competition.

**Article 36.** Arbiters shall monitor boards that are low on time to prevent emergencies and assist players when help is needed (such as handling protests or supplying additional scoresheets).

**Article 37.** An arbiter may pause players' clocks when necessary, such as when a player lodges a protest.

**Article 38.** Arbiters have the authority to impose penalties on players or spectators proportionate to the offense, as follows:
1. Warning
2. Forfeit the offending player and award the win to the opponent
3. Double forfeit (forfeit both sides)
4. Financial fine (must be announced in advance with posted notices, e.g., penalty for smoking inside the playing hall)
5. Expulsion from the competition

---

### 2.6) Appeals

**Article 39.** When a player protests to an arbiter and disputes the resulting decision, the player may appeal to the Appeals Committee.

**Article 40.** An appeal is lodged by depositing **2,000 THB** with the tournament organizing committee before the next round of competition begins.

**Article 41.** The Appeals Committee consists of **3 qualified individuals** to adjudicate the dispute. They need not be arbiters at the event and may even be participants, provided they meet all of the following qualifications:
1. Generally recognized as knowledgeable and expert in the rules of 8-piece Thai Draughts.
2. Present at the competition.
3. Have no conflict of interest with either party (e.g., not a coach of either side, not the arbiter whose ruling is being appealed, and not a parent/guardian of either party).

**Article 42.** If the Appeals Committee overturns the arbiter's decision, the deposit shall be refunded to the appellant and the Appeals Committee's ruling shall be enforced.

If the Appeals Committee upholds the arbiter's decision, the deposit is forfeited to the tournament organizer.

## Chapter 3: Appendices for 8-Piece Thai Draughts

### 3.1) Move Notation

**Article 43.** Moves are recorded using the square numbers on the board, for example:
- `8 – 11` means moving from square `8` to square `11`.
- `5 x 14` means capturing from square `5` to square `14`.

**Article 44.** For multi-captures, record only the starting square and the final landing square. For example, in a 3-piece capture moving from `5` to `14`, then `23`, then `30`, the long-form notation would be `5 x 14 x 23 x 30`, **but in official game recording it shall be written as `5 x 30`**.

---

### 3.2) Time Controls

**Article 45.** **Standard:** A game in which each player starts with at least **12 minutes** plus a **5-second increment** per move starting from move 1.

**Article 46.** **Rapid:** A game in which each player starts with **5 minutes** plus a **3-second increment** per move starting from move 1.

**Article 47.** **Blitz:** A game in which each player starts with **3 minutes** plus a **2-second increment** per move starting from move 1.

---

### 3.3) Competition Formats

**Article 48.** Unless otherwise specified in tournament regulations, individual competitions award **1 point for a win**, **0.5 points for a draw**, and **0 points for a loss**.

**Article 49.** **Swiss System Pairing:** A format that pairs players with equal (or closest) scores round after round through all scheduled rounds. All players play every round without elimination, and final standings are ranked from highest to lowest total score. The number of rounds depends on the number of entrants in the division—the more entrants, the more rounds should be played.

This format is unsuitable for very small fields such as 5–8 players or 5–8 teams, because pairing in later rounds becomes impossible or very difficult.

**Article 50.** **Knockout:** A format where players are paired head-to-head; the loser is eliminated and the winner advances to the next round until one final champion remains who has won every round. A 3rd–4th place playoff between semifinal losers may also be held.

**Article 51.** **Round-Robin:** A format where every player faces every other player in the group/division, and standings are determined by cumulative points. Best suited for tournaments with a relatively small number of participants.

**Article 52.** **Group Stage:** A format that divides players into groups and ranks players within each group to advance to the next stage. Ratings, seeding, or other distribution methods must be used to balance playing strength so top players are not concentrated in a single group and the competition remains fair; if this cannot be done, another format should be used.

**Article 53.** **Hybrid Format:** A format combining multiple competition systems, for example: a Swiss preliminary stage from which the top 4 players advance to a Knockout semifinal and final.

**Article 54.** The tournament organizer may adopt whichever competition format is appropriate.

---

### 3.4) Tiebreaks

**Article 55.** A **tiebreak** is a secondary score used to break ties when players finish with the same primary tournament score. If the first tiebreak is also tied, the 2nd, 3rd, 4th tiebreaks, etc., are applied in order.

**Recommended tiebreaks for Individual Swiss tournaments:**
1. Direct Encounter
2. Buchholz
3. Sonneborn-Berger
4. Minor score
5. Playoff

**Recommended tiebreaks for Individual Round-Robin tournaments:**
1. Direct Encounter
2. Sonneborn-Berger *(numbered `5.` in the original Thai PDF)*
3. Minor score
4. Playoff

**Recommended tiebreaks for Team Swiss tournaments:**  
Primary score: Match points
1. Direct Encounter
2. Game points
3. Sonneborn-Berger
4. Minor score
5. Playoff

**Recommended tiebreaks for Team Round-Robin tournaments:**  
Primary score: Match points
1. Direct Encounter
2. Game points
3. Sonneborn-Berger
4. Minor score
5. Playoff

**Article 56.** **Direct Encounter:** The head-to-head result between the tied players. Suitable as the first tiebreak in any format that uses tiebreaks.

**Article 57.** **Buchholz:** The sum of the final net scores of all opponents a player faced during the tournament.

**Article 58.** **Sonneborn-Berger:** The sum of the final net scores of opponents faced, differing from Buchholz in that a player receives the opponent's full score for a win, half of the opponent's score for a draw, and nothing for a loss.

For team events, Sonneborn-Berger is calculated using team Match points.

*(Note from original text: Buchholz is used in Swiss systems and not in Round-Robin, because in Round-Robin players with equal scores would have identical Buchholz sums and it cannot differentiate standings.)*

**Article 59.** **Number of Wins:** The total number of games won in that tournament.

**Article 60.** **Minor Score:** The total across all sub-boards in the match/tournament scoring **3 for a win**, **1 for a draw**, and **0 for a loss**.

**Article 61.** **Match Point (Team Score):** The team's score for a round. Commonly used as the primary score, though it may serve as a tiebreak if Game points are used as the primary score.

Calculation: sum the individual Game points of all players on the team in that round. If higher than the opposing team's sum, the team wins and earns **2 Match points**; if equal, the match is drawn and each team earns **1 Match point**; if lower, the team loses and earns **0 Match points**.

Examples:
- In a 3-player team with 1 win, 1 draw, and 1 loss, the team draws the round and earns 1 Match point.
- In a 2-player team with 1 loss and 1 draw, the team loses the round and earns 0 Match points.
- In a 3-player team where all 3 players win, the team wins the round and earns 2 Match points.

**Article 62.** **Game Point (Individual Score):** The points scored by each player on a team: **1 point for a win**, **0.5 points for a draw**, and **0 points for a loss**. For example, in a 3-board match with 1 win and 1 draw (and 1 loss), the team scores 1.5 Game points for that round.

Game points can be used as the primary score, though in 8-piece Thai Draughts team competitions **Match points** should be used as the primary score, making Game points a tiebreak following Direct Encounter.

Examples:
- In a 3-player team with 1 win, 1 draw, and 1 loss, the team earns 1.5 Game points for the round.
- In a 2-player team with 1 loss and 1 draw, the team earns 0.5 Game points for the round.
- In a 3-player team where all 3 players win, the team earns 3 Game points for the round.

### 3.5) Team Competitions

**Article 63.** Unless otherwise specified in tournament regulations, team competitions award **2 points for a team win**, **1 point for a draw**, and **0 points for a loss**.

**Article 64.** In team competitions with multiple players per team, teams must submit their roster prior to the start of the tournament, including the Fixed Board Order and reserve players.

**Article 65.** There are 2 types of board order: **Fixed Board Order** and **Round Board Order**.

- **Fixed Board Order:** The initial board order of the team's players, which must be submitted to the arbiter before play begins and cannot have names or ordering changed until the tournament ends—except that the organizer may allow lineup changes in knockout/playoff rounds if applied uniformly to all competing teams.
- **Round Board Order:** The board order submitted for a specific round. The organizer pairs players board-by-board according to the Round Board Order submitted by each team prior to that round.

After team pairings are published, teams must submit their Round Board Order by the specified deadline so the organizer can prepare board pairings before the round starts. In Round 1 only, if time is insufficient, the organizer may permit using the Fixed Board Order.

If a team fails to submit a Round Board Order for a round, its Fixed Board Order shall be used for pairing in that round.

**Article 66.** Systems for rotating/substituting board orders include:
1. **Shift-Up System:** If a player does not play in a given round, their name is removed and the remaining players shift up in order according to the Fixed Board Order.
2. **Free-Order System:** Players listed in the Fixed Board Order may freely rearrange their board order on the Round Board Order form.
3. **Hybrid System:** Combines multiple systems—for example, locking Board 1 while allowing lower boards to rotate. Commonly used in events where a single competition produces both individual and team standings.

**Article 67.** The number of rounds shall be determined by the tournament organizer as appropriate for the number of participating entries.

### 3.6) Tiebreak Playoff Boards (Special Boards)

When a decisive winner-and-loser result is required in a round that ended in a draw, the following special playoff board rules apply in sequence:

**Article 68.** After a regular match ends in a draw, play 8-piece Thai Draughts where the **first-moving side must win (a draw counts as a loss for the first mover)**, alternating who moves first, at **12 minutes + 5 seconds increment** from move 1, for **2 games**.

**Article 69.** If still tied after Article 68, play 8-piece Thai Draughts where the **second-moving side must win (a draw counts as a loss for the second mover)**, alternating who moves second, at **12 minutes + 5 seconds increment** from move 1, for **2 games**.

**Article 70.** If still tied after Articles 68 and 69, play 8-piece Thai Draughts where the **first-moving side must win (a draw counts as a loss for the first mover)**, alternating who moves first, at **5 minutes + 3 seconds increment** from move 1, for **2 games**.

**Article 71.** If still tied after Articles 68, 69, and 70, play 8-piece Thai Draughts where the **second-moving side must win (a draw counts as a loss for the second mover)**, alternating who moves second, at **5 minutes + 3 seconds increment** from move 1, for **2 games**.

**Article 72.** If still tied after Articles 68–71, play 8-piece Thai Draughts where the **first-moving side must win (a draw counts as a loss for the first mover)**, alternating who moves first, at **3 minutes + 2 seconds increment** from move 1, for **2 games**.

**Article 73.** If still tied after Articles 68–72, play 8-piece Thai Draughts where the **second-moving side must win (a draw counts as a loss for the second mover)**, alternating who moves second, at **3 minutes + 2 seconds increment** from move 1, for **2 games**.

**Article 74.** If still tied after Articles 68–73, add 4 more Men per side to play **12-piece Draughts** as the decider under the same rules as 8-piece Thai Draughts (Armageddon format). One side receives **12 minutes + 5 seconds increment** from move 1 and **must win (a draw counts as a loss)**; the other side receives **5 minutes + 3 seconds increment** from move 1 and **only needs a draw (a draw counts as a win)**, played as **1 game**.

Lots are drawn to decide which player takes the "must-win (draw = loss)" side, and that player moves first.

---

### 3.7) Special Rules for Players with Disabilities

**Article 75.** Visually impaired players may touch the pieces to determine which pieces are on which squares, and are exempt from the touch-move rule requiring them to move a touched piece; alternatively, an assistant may handle move recording, announcing moves, and moving pieces on their behalf, as appropriate.

All such accommodations shall align with the regulations established by the Sports Association for the Disabled.
